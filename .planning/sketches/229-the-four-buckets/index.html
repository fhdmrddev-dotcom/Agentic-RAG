229 · The four buckets

Phase 233 · PREV-01 / LIB-09. A person points at a Drive folder. Before anything is imported they must see four honestly-labelled lists — and the fourth one, “can’t tell without reading it”, is the one every tidy design deletes.

SC#1, verbatim: “A person points at a Drive folder and sees four lists before anything is imported: what would be added, what is already here (matched by source file, not by content), what type is not supported, and what cannot be told without reading it.”

So the deciding axis is whether a bucket can DISAPPEAR. All three variants show four buckets at rest. Only some of them still show four after the user touches something. “A bucket is dropped to make the screen tidier — three buckets is the version that lies” is a named failure mode in the ROADMAP, and it is the reading that separates these three.

Why it holds
The four buckets are structure. There is no chip to unselect, no accordion to collapse to zero, no “show only new”. A screenshot of this surface at any moment contains all four counts — so SC#1 is met by construction rather than by audit.
The animation is the argument
Files fly from the scanner head into a lane, one at a time. Three lanes settle. The fourth keeps its hatched ground and its breathing count — you watch the preview decline to guess. That is the honesty claim made kinetic instead of written in a caption nobody reads.
The cost
Four columns at ~300px each needs ≥1080px. Below that it wraps 2×2 — still four, never three. Long filenames wrap rather than truncate: deliberate, because a truncated name in the already here lane is exactly how you mis-identify a file.

What it gets right
One row per file, one verdict per row — the densest read, and it maps onto a table the app already ships. The sweep resolves each verdict in place, so you see the pass happen without a layout change.
Why it fails the clause
A filter chip is a control that removes a bucket. Turn off “can’t tell without reading it” — the count goes with it and the surface reads as a clean three-way split. Try it above. That state is reachable in one click and survives a screenshot, a screen-share and a handover.
Salvageable?
Only by making the chips non-interactive — at which point they are lane headers with extra steps, i.e. Variant A. Keep the table shape for the row; drop the filter semantics.

The one thing it does better than A
Proportion. A count of 4 next to a count of 12 is arithmetic; a hatched segment holding a visible sixth of the bar is a feeling. And the bar is a single object — a screenshot of it always sums to 26.
The compromise
Below the bar the buckets are accordions, and an accordion can be collapsed. That is weaker than A’s lanes but stronger than B’s filters: collapsing hides the files, never the bucket or its count.
What survives into the build
The bar. It is ~40 lines, it belongs above A’s lanes, and it makes the fourth bucket proportionally undeniable at a glance before anyone has read a single lane header.

Recommendation — A as the control, C’s bar folded in as its header

  • A Four lanes. The buckets are structure, not state. There is no interaction that produces a three-bucket screen, so the “no bucket disappears” clause is met by construction — the same reading that decided sketch 228.
  • C The proportional bar only, mounted above A’s lanes. Proportion is the one thing counts cannot convey, and the hatched segment does more honest work than any label. Its accordions are dropped; A’s lanes replace them.
  • B Rejected on the criterion, not on taste. Filter chips are a control that removes a bucket. Its table shape is worth keeping for the row (file · verdict · where/why); its filter semantics are not.

What this sketch commits to

  • The four labels are verbatim deliverables. “Already here” always carries matched by source file, not by content — because documents.py:620 hashes raw bytes, which needs the download this preview exists to avoid, and Drive publishes no hash at all for native Docs/Sheets/Slides.
  • Every row in the fourth bucket names its own reason. A bucket labelled “can’t tell” whose rows do not say why we cannot tell is a shrug, not a disclosure.
  • Motion is load-bearing, not decorative. Three lanes settle; the fourth keeps breathing. Remove the animation and the surface is still honest — remove the fourth lane and it is not.
  • Tier 1 identity only. The scan compares (source_system, external_id, source_version) for equality and never calls it a hash. Tier 2 sha256 still runs at splice.
  • Zero writes, stated on the surface. Every variant carries the 0 documents · 0 chunks · 0 jobs footer. Sketch 230 turns that line into the argument.
