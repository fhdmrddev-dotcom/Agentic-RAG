§4 · What the picker prevents — and why validating afterwards is not enough

The failure SC#5 names in its own words: "an invented step that validates and fails at 03:00."

without the picker · validate afterwards
1 · The author writes a sentence naming Salesforce.
2 · The model drafts a workflow with a Salesforce step, because nothing told it not to.
3 · A post-draft check finds the step and removes it — or does not, because it is a model-behaviour guarantee.
4 · The workflow publishes.
5 · 03:00 Monday. The step runs and fails.

⚠ Step 3 is the whole problem. A guarantee that depends on the model noticing is not a guarantee — and the step reached step 4 having validated.

with the picker · structurally impossible
1 · The author writes a sentence naming Salesforce.
2 · The generator's vocabulary is the ticked set, and Salesforce is not in it.
3 · The door refuses before drafting, names the service, and offers two real next actions.
4 · Nothing is drafted, nothing is published, nothing runs at 03:00.

⭐ The grant grain is the vocabulary grain. Not merely connected services — granted tools, which is why STEP-06's stated dependency is Phase 213.

What the refusal must contain — the ROADMAP's own test

"A refusal is only honest if it names the next action." This one names two, and both are real: one leaves for Settings and comes back; one stays here. ⚠ A third arm — connecting the service inline, without leaving the door — was rejected: a connection is a credential, and a credential form on a drafting screen is a new outbound trust surface with no prior review cycle.
