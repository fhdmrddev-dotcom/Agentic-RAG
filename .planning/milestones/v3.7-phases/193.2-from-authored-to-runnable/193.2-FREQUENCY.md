## §4 Attribution — stated honestly (OQ-4)

**The phase claims a COMBINED effect and attributes nothing.** Plan `193.2-05` made **two** edits
inside one string literal — the DELIVERABLE-RULE suppression clause **and** the `llm_human_input`
bullet shortened from 121 to 74 characters (its active tail, *"use for any 'confirm before finalizing'
step"*, removed). A single measurement cannot say which of the two moved figure 2, or whether both
did.

**The third arm that would have attributed it was NOT driven.** It would have needed the clause with
the ORIGINAL 121-character bullet restored — a temporary edit to production source, four more rows and
twenty more paid calls, to answer a question no success criterion asks. The operator's authorization
was explicitly for the **control**, not for resolution on attribution. So: **not driven, and said
plainly rather than implied.**

`grounding.py:605-607` records exactly this lesson in its own code — *"One home per concern: the rule
states the policy, the grounding states the FACTS the policy reads"* — which is why `193.2-05`
deliberately left `grounding.py` alone. That discipline is what makes figure 3 above a valid control at
all: the D-26 arms are on an unchanged input, so a fall in figure 3 could only have come from the
prompt change.

## §5 What this does NOT prove

1. **It is a frequency, not a guarantee.** k/N over N = 5 per arm is a sample. The field can still
   arrive blank and the interactive step can still appear; when they do, the fallback is **today's
   exact behaviour** (D-08). **The publish gate stays.**
2. **It does not prove a template gets FILLED.** Figure 3 counts a `render_template` *phase in a
   draft*. 193.1 learned the hard way that a 3,600-case suite could not see the assumption that
   mattered; only the manual UAT row (re-running the kit end to end and scoring planted facts) can say
   the document comes back filled.
3. **It does not prove the env-only knob works in a running backend.** `harness_authoring_model` has
   no UI and needs a restart; the `gpt-5.5` row was reached through the harness's in-process stub, not
   through a live backend. That remains manual (the 187 analog's row M7).
4. **It says nothing about the six N/A providers.** They were not driven, on purpose, and their rows
   are reasons — not results.
5. **It is one describe per arm.** A different phrasing could compose differently.

## §6 Three things measured that the phase's own documents get wrong

Recorded here because a correction living only in a run log is invisible.

**(a) The plan's "same artefact" premise is false.** Covered in §3 — 193.1's 3/3 was the eight-key
set, not the kit's ten-field `.docx`. Anyone later comparing a ten-field figure to 3/3 as an equality
would be comparing two different things.

**(b) ⚠ `193.1-11-SUMMARY.md`'s `inputs[]` conclusion is TRUE OF ANTHROPIC AND FALSE AS A GENERAL
CLAIM — and this matters to plan `193.2-09`.** It measured `inputs[] is null` on **6 of 6** and told
plan 09 that D-20's run-input bucket *"is fed by nothing"*, so its degenerate-first design must not be
softened. Measured here across 20 calls:

| Row | `inputs[]` declared | keys, when declared |
|---|---|---|
| anthropic `kit10` | **0/5** | — |
| anthropic `uat8` | **0/5** | — |
| **openai `kit10`** | **2/5** | `client_name`, `review_period` |
| **openai `uat8`** | **5/5** | `project_name`, `reporting_period`, `stakeholder_audience` |

**Anthropic reproduces 193.1 exactly (0/10). `gpt-5.5` feeds the bucket on 7 of 10, with keys that ARE
placeholder names.** ⚠ **This is a PROVIDER difference, not an effect of this phase's prompt change** —
193.1 never drove `gpt-5.5`, so nothing here attributes the change to 193.2, and the claim is stated as
a provider observation rather than as a fix. **Plan 09 must still survive the degenerate case** (it is
what anthropic, the model that actually resolves, produces every time); it should simply stop believing
that the bucket can never be fed.

**(c) ⚠ The D-07 durability instruction is only partly obeyed, and the provenance mark cannot see
that.** The prompt asks for a requirement *"phrased so it stays true for the next run and the one
after, NOT a restatement of the particular request"*. On the `kit10` arm:

- **anthropic — 0 of 5** requirements named a one-run parameter. Representative: *"Produce a completed
  quarterly business review document for **a named customer and quarter**, grounded in the knowledge
  base … with any unavailable facts stated plainly rather than invented."* Durable, and it even carries
  the clause's no-inventing instruction.
- **openai — 5 of 5** named one. Representative: *"Produce a quarterly business review for customer
  **Northwind Logistics** covering **Q3 2026** …"* — one run's parameters baked into the workflow's
  definition of done, which is precisely what D-07 asks against.

**All 20 were still stamped `seeded_by_ai: True`, and that is CORRECT rather than a bug.** The stamp's
question is *"is this a normalised copy of the describe text?"* — it is not, so the mark is earned.
`193.2-07` explicitly rejected a fuzzy similarity metric, with its reason recorded in
`_normalised_for_copy_check`'s own docstring: a threshold would refuse provenance for requirements that
are genuinely durable but share vocabulary with the describe box, which is the ordinary case. **The
finding is therefore about READING the mark, not about fixing it: the AI-proposal mark means "a model
wrote this", never "this is durable".** Plan `193.2-09`'s copy must not imply the latter, and an author
reviewing an openai-authored requirement has something real to edit.
