### 2. [Rule 1 — the plan's single-derivation instruction would have SHIPPED A LIE]

The plan says the step count comes from *"plan 06 task 1's derivation over `frame.phases` (stopped
arm) or `usePhases(threadId)` (live arm)"*. **Measured, applying one derivation to both is wrong,
and wrong in the most dangerous direction.**

`lib/phaseState.ts:59-72` maps the DB literal `completed` to the CLIENT member **`done`**. So
`usePhases` returns rows whose `status` is `done`, and `stepsFrom` counts `=== "completed"`. The
call **TYPECHECKS** — `stepsFrom` takes a structural `{status: string}` — and then reports
**`0 of 3` on a run where three phases finished**: precisely the "nothing survived" reading 194
D-13 forbids, arrived at through the helper written to prevent it.

**Fix:** two vocabularies, two **SHIPPED** derivations, zero hand-rolled mappings — `stepsFrom`
over the WIRE (stopped arm), `harnessBannerProgress` (`lib/toolMeta.ts:102`) over the STORE (live
arm). A case asserts the live reading (`Step 2 of 3`) with a comment naming the trap by name, and
`runStepCount.ts`'s docblock records it as a binding rule.

**Secondary correction:** `usePhases` was *not* used even for the live arm. It wraps
`usePanelReconcile`, which fires **its own fetch** — this line already owns one read and must not
acquire a second by accident. The `phasesByThread` slice is read directly, with a module-scope
`EMPTY_PHASES` so the selector returns a stable reference (the churn hazard
`StreamsProvider.tsx:3293` records by name).

### 5. [Rule 1 — my own fence] The SQL comment stripper stripped NOTHING, on CRLF

The migration parser strips `--` comments before harvesting literals, because migration 119's
`ARRAY[…]` block interleaves comments — one of which quotes a rendered sentence. The obvious
`line.replace(/--.*$/, "")` **matched nothing at all**: `supabase/migrations/*.sql` carry **CRLF**
terminators (`file` reports it; `od -c` shows `\r \n`), and JavaScript's `.` excludes `\r`, so
`--.*$` can never reach end-of-string. **A comment stripper that strips nothing is worse than
none** — the parse downstream reads prose as schema and reports green.

Caught by the case rather than by reading it. Now `/--[^\r\n]*/g`, and the case asserts the
stripped text is genuinely **shorter** and that the quoted sentence `no deliverable produced` is
present in the raw file and absent from the stripped one — so it measures the strip's REACH, not
merely its existence.
