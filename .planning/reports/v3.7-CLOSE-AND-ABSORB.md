# v3.7 — Close Cleanly, Absorb Deliberately

**A business-perspective assessment of the workflow product: what ships, what does not, and what to do about
it inside the four phases that remain.**

**Date:** 2026-08-16 · **Branch:** `develop` · **Author:** orchestrator, from two parallel research passes
(debt inventory · capability map), both instructed to measure rather than inherit.

---

## The question this answers

The operator's words:

> *"This workflow work has taken too much longer and we have other things to do. At least we can close the
> milestone cleanly, but we can absorb some things in the workflow rather than dedicating another milestone
> only for workflow."*

So: **what is outstanding, what fits in phases 195–198, and what must wait — such that v3.7 closes honestly
rather than quietly growing a second workflow milestone.**

---

## 1. The one-paragraph answer

**The engine does one thing well and one thing only.** Measured against the six capability axes the business
cases demand (`SEED-168`), it expresses exactly **one position on each**: run it on demand, from scratch,
by retrieving and summarizing, with a single approve-or-reject, into one templated document, with citations.
That column is real and sellable. It is also the column that **fails in week two**, when someone says *"update
the one from last week"* — and every domain surveyed hits that wall at the same point.

**The good news is that the gap is not where it looked.** Three of the most valuable missing capabilities turn
out to be *one function*, *two files*, and *one deleted gate* away — not subsystems. **The single cheapest
change in this whole report unlocks per-document processing with no engine change at all.**

**Recommendation: close v3.7 on schedule with four phases as scoped, absorb ten specific items, and do NOT
open a second workflow milestone.** What genuinely remains is one coherent *automations* milestone, later,
with a clear boundary.

---

## 2. What the business actually needs — and how much of it we have

The product's job, stated once:

> **Automate standardized work whose input is organizational knowledge and whose output a person must be
> willing to stand behind.**

Surveying real cases across **PMO, HR, finance, legal, education and healthcare** produced a finding that
should shape the roadmap more than any individual feature: **five shapes cover all six domains** — *maintain a
register · produce a periodic report · check a corpus against a standard · assemble an evidence pack · watch
for what is due or missing.* There is no need to "do HR" or "do legal". There is a need to express five shapes.

### Coverage, measured against shipped source and the live database

| Axis | What the business needs | What ships | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Temporal** — what starts it | on demand · on a cadence · on an event · on a date condition | on demand only | ⚠ **A2/A3 absent. But A4's date CONDITION already ships** (`within_next` / `older_than` / `before` / `after` / `between`) and is already exposed to the model — only the clock is missing |
| **B · State** — what it knows about last time | stateless · **incremental update** · append-only · reconciliation | stateless only | ⚠ **Zero run-to-run memory.** Confirmed across a ten-spelling search plus every plausible back door. The declared `output_target_folder` / `reingest_output` / `version_policy` fields have **no consumers at all** |
| **C · Evidence** — how it reads the KB | summarize · **per-document extraction** · compare to a reference · absence detection | summarize only | ⚠ **`kind:"reference"` assets have ZERO consumers** — a playbook can be attached and is never read. **But C2 is one function away** (§4) |
| **D · Human role** — what the person does | approve · **review-and-edit** · **item-level adjudication** · supply judgement · sign-off | approve only — and hollow | ⚠ **Zero author adoption**: armed on 5 phases, all `external_action`, all by structural coercion. ## 3. The honest state of the milestone

**v3.7 requirements:** 5 of 13 satisfied. Ticked — LIB-01…05 and AUTH-03. Remaining — AUTH-01, AUTH-02,
AUTH-04, RUN-01, RUN-02, RUN-03, NODE-01, NODE-02.

**The blocker on closing honestly is RUN-01**, and it is one line of SQL away from resolvable:

- Phase 194 shipped the Stop control; 194.1 made it visible on four surfaces; **the operator drove five UAT
  rows today and all five passed.**
- But at the shipped `WORKER_COUNT=2`, **roughly half of stops still end with the run reporting `completed`**
  — because `finish_run` is an unconditional `UPDATE workflow_runs SET status=$2 WHERE id=$1` with **no
  terminal guard**.

⚠ **The two halves must not be sold as one.** A terminal guard on `finish_run` makes the run **report**

say plainly that the second is outstanding.**

**AUTH-01 is the milestone's only unmet requirement with no phase working on it** — the two authoring doors
still share 11 of 14 first-screen text nodes; the operator's words were *"they look the same exactly to me."*

## 4. The four highest-leverage changes — ranked by axes-unlocked per unit of cost

This is the section that decides whether a second milestone is needed. **It is not.**

### ① Open the `programmatic` registry — *one function, no engine change*

The registry holds **two** functions today, one of which is a test sleep. Everything around it ships: the
decorator, the executor, input resolution, and — critically — `_exec_programmatic` **already spreads a prior
phase's entire output dict into the function's input**, so a registered function can read the structured
`field_map` right now.

⚠ **The measured consequence:** `_collect_sub_questions` accepts a `sub_questions` list from **any** prior
phase output, and an agent phase's `source_refs` is already `[{document_id, filename}]`. **So one function —
read `source_refs`, return one task per document — makes the existing batch-agent phase fan out over a
DOCUMENT SET today.** That is per-document extraction (axis C2), the shape half the legal and finance cases
need, unlocked by one function in one file.

The same lever then reaches **C4** (a set-difference function is the first absence primitive), **E3** (mint
stable row ids), and **B2/B4** (the deterministic diff/merge). ⚠ It also fixes a quieter defect: the authoring
prompt tells the AI it may use *"a deterministic registered function"* **without naming a single one**.

### ② Replace publish stage 2.5 — *unblocks the entire human axis*

The gate refuses **two** shapes: any human-input phase, **and any validator that escalates to a human**. The
problem it exists to solve — a publish-time golden run must not block on a person — **already has a shipped
two-line solution** used by the armed checkpoint: auto-continue when `is_golden_run`. Apply the same gate and
the categorical refusal becomes unnecessary.

Unlocks **D2, D4 and F2's escalation arm** at once, and makes approve/reject authorable as a plain step
instead of a side effect of an outbound action.

⚠ **This MUST ship together with the timeout fix** (`BUG-260816-06`). Widening the gate without it publishes
workflows whose approval step silently self-approves after five minutes. **Shipping ② alone is a regression,
not a feature.**

### ③ Per-phase template binding — *two files, additive-optional, no migration*

Multi-artifact output is blocked because binding is definition-level and first-match-wins. Adding an optional
asset field to the emit phase config unlocks **E4** outright, in the same additive-optional shape all seven
config types already use.

### ④ A prior-run handle as a run input — *the biggest of the four, still not a subsystem*

Persist a `prior_run_id` alongside the `kickoff_prompt` and folder that runs already carry; add one function
that loads that run's structured output. Unlocks **B2/B3**, and with ①'s diff function, **B4** — the entire
state axis, and with it the *"update last week's"* case that otherwise kills every domain.

⚠ **The tempting shortcut is worse:** wiring the dead `reingest_output` field so outputs re-enter the
knowledge base gets state "free" via retrieval — with no preservation guarantee, which is precisely the
failure mode that makes a register untrustworthy.

**Deliberately unranked: item-level adjudication (D3).** It needs ① *and* ② *and* a new card contract — the
axis position with the most prerequisites. **Worth knowing before phase 198 scopes NODE-02 around it.**

## 7. Process debt found on the way — worth more than one bug

Three findings about the *records* rather than the code. This project's own rule is that **a bug's `status:`
frontmatter IS the routing index**, and it failed three ways today:

1. ⚠ **Three duplicate bug IDs**, all minted 2026-08-16 — **my error**, caught by the inventory pass.
   `REQUIREMENTS.md` instructed *"read BUG-260816-02 at discuss-phase 195"* while that id resolved to **two
   different reports with different statuses**. Renumbered and repointed. *A routing instruction naming an
   ambiguous id is worse than none, because it reads as precise.*
2. ⚠ **Four reports marked `folded` whose own bodies say a half stays open** — and `BUG-260610-01` did it
   twice in one day: folded into 194.1 at discuss time, **refuted by the operator's screenshot hours later**,
   re-opened. Both times the pattern was identical: *a claim recorded as an outcome.*
3. ⚠ **Two major UAT findings existed only inside one phase's UAT file**, invisible to every routing scan.
   Now filed.

**Good news worth stating:** all four deferral registers (192 → 194.1) carry a re-open trigger on **every**
entry — including two that state *"no trigger, by construction"* explicitly, so a scan cannot misread them as
omissions. The deferral discipline is working; the **reported-bugs index** is where the leak is.
