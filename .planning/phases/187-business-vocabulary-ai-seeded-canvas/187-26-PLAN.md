<design_decision>
**The plan settles this; the executor does not re-open it.**

GAP A had two candidate shapes. **Shape (i) — a knowledge-base picker on the describe door, threaded
through the existing handoff so the definition is born bound — is chosen.**

Why (i):
- The sketch-approved fast path's promise is **speed**. A picker is a control the author may ignore;
  it costs one glance and zero clicks. It does not convert a one-click path into a gate.
- D-187-11's own wording treats an unbound workflow as *"the author has not yet said what this is
  about"* — a **state worth surfacing rather than forbidding**. That sentence is only true if the
  author had somewhere to say it. Shape (i) creates that somewhere; shape (ii) does not.
- It reuses the seam that already exists. The Builder already has a describe-screen picker, an
  `onDraft` that already accepts `project_folder_id`, and a handoff that already carries text. Shape
  (i) adds one wire; shape (ii) adds a new state machine to `autoDraft`.

Why **not** (ii) — *stop `autoDraft` generating until the binding question is answered or declined*:
- It **delays** the draft without ever letting the author bind: after the pause they still land on
  a Builder whose picker is on a screen `autoDraft` skipped. It fixes the symptom's timing, not the
  missing control.
- It fights `autoDraft`'s once-only ref and its `builderPhase === "empty"` guard, which is exactly
  the kind of control-flow change on a shared path this phase has rejected twice (D-187-01's
  rejection of fix shape B, verbatim reasoning).
- A blocking question on the door whose entire recorded purpose is *"loose · fastest path"* is a
  scope reduction of a shipped, sketch-approved surface.

**They are not complementary and (ii) is NOT additionally scoped.** Once the author can bind on the
door, a gate adds nothing but friction.

**The picker is OPTIONAL by construction.** Nothing about the CTA's enablement changes. An author
who ignores it gets today's behaviour exactly, and the `unbound_retrieval` verdict then means what
D-187-11 says it means.
</design_decision>
