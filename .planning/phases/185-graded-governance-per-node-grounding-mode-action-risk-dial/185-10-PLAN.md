<objective>
Draw the armed action-risk checkpoint on the canvas as a DETOUR: the connector into the risky step leaves
the flow and comes back through a point that represents the person.

Purpose: SPEC Req 8's amendment (sketch 147, three rounds, operator-locked). It is the only one of three
finalists that says what the engine actually does — the run stops, hands out to a human, and resumes —
rather than "blocked", which is what both rejected rounds of barrier shapes said. Arming happens in the
side panel; the canvas mark is READ-ONLY, because `PhaseNodeCard.tsx:37-48` forbids any focusable control
inside a node card. The panel is where you SET, the canvas is where you SEE.

**D-185-18 is the load-bearing correction and this plan exists because of it.** SPEC Req 8's build note
says the detour "rides the `edgeTypes` entry named `flow` that `WorkflowCanvas.tsx:279` already registers
— no net-new canvas infrastructure." **That entry does not exist.** `grep -rn edgeTypes frontend/src`
returns exactly one hit: a comment at `:276-279` stating the OPPOSITE — *"setting `edge.type` would make
the library look up an `edgeTypes` entry named `flow` and fall back with a warning, since 183 registers
none."* `canvasModel.ts` sets `data.kind` on all four edge-push sites and never sets `type`. So this is
four net-new pieces, and setting `type` switches EVERY flow edge from the library's default renderer to
ours. No estimate may treat this as "just wire the data".

Output: `FlowEdge.tsx`, a module-scope `edgeTypes` map, `edge.type` on flow edges, `markerEnd` re-rendered
inside the custom edge, and a no-visible-change guard on ordinary edges.
