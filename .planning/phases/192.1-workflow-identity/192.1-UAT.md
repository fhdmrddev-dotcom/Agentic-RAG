## What shipped, in the operator's vocabulary

The **identity line** is a new second line on every workflow card, at DOM position 2 — under the
name, above the folder chip. It renders, in this order:

```
[Yours | Shared] · [discriminator] · [discriminator] [1 of N] · changed <when>
```

- **`Yours` / `Shared`** — whose it is.
- **discriminators** — computed per row from what actually differs among the rows sharing its name.
  **At most two, ever.** They are drawn from `Copy of <parent>` / `v3 of v2` / `Original`,
  the project, `Ready to run` / `Still building` / `Shared starter`, and `v3`.
- **`1 of N`** — appears **only** when the name collides. `N` counts the whole library, not the
  filtered view, so it does not change while you type.
- **`changed <when>`** — nine bands: `just now` · `N min ago` · `N hours ago` · `yesterday` ·
  `N days ago` · `last week` · `N weeks ago` · `last month` · `N months ago`.

A row with a **unique** name still carries the line, but carries **no discriminators** — lineage
only if it really is a fork, plus the ownership word and `changed`.

The **fork verb** (`Make my own copy`) now opens a prompt titled **`Name your copy`**, whose primary
button reads **`Create my copy`** — never `Confirm`.

## ⚠ TWO THINGS YOU WILL SEE THAT ARE **NOT** BUGS — read these before driving

These are this phase's own residuals. They are **written here so they are reported rather than
discovered**, which is the difference between a known trade and a defect.

### Residual 1 — the 43-name family is NOT fully solved, and `1 of N` is telling the truth about it

On the operator's real *Compliance Gap Report* family, the rows are identical in **every fact the
product holds**: same lineage phrase, no project at all (their `definition` is a `SEED-154` string
scalar, so the project axis is empty for all of them), and a state axis that splits only 41/3.

**No ranker can separate rows that are identical in every recorded fact.** Measured in `192.1-05`
against the real family's shape:

| | largest block of byte-identical identity lines |
|---|---|
| the approved sketch's `varies()` rule | **41** |
| the shipped D-31 ranker | **39** |

Two rows. That is the whole improvement on this family, and it is asserted as a test with a
**`>= 35` FLOOR** — a floor that FAILS if a future change makes the number look better by inventing
a technical discriminator (a slug hash, an id fragment, a created-at). That was explicitly forbidden;
it is the vocabulary drift the 187 node-face ladder rules out.

**So `1 of N` on those rows is not a failure of the line. It IS the line's answer:** *this row is not
distinguishable from its namesakes, and I will not pretend otherwise.* **The product's answer to that
is to give it a name** — which is exactly what the new fork prompt (sketch 162-B) ships, and what
**U5** drives.

**Where SC#1 IS judged:** on the realistic generated fixture, where the axes genuinely differ. There
the line produces **75 distinct renderings across 106 rows**, **24 distinct discriminator strips
across the 93 colliding rows**, and a largest byte-identical block of **5**. Both figures are
published; neither may be quoted as the other.

### Residual 2 — the family's TRUE ORIGINAL shows NO lineage, while its copies say `Copy of …`

`compliance-gap-report` — the base slug of the 43-row family — **is itself orphan-shaped**, because
`report` happens to be six legal lowercase alphanumerics and therefore matches the copy-suffix rule
`^(.*)-[a-z0-9]{6}$`. Its parent slug does not exist, so it resolves to the fourth lineage state:
**silence**.

On screen that reads:

| Row | Lineage segment |
|---|---|
| `compliance-gap-report` **v1 starter** — the family's real original | **nothing at all** |
| its ~43 copies | `Copy of Compliance Gap Report starter` |

**This is CORRECT under D-13 and it will look like a bug unless you have read this paragraph.** The
alternative was to render `Original`, which on a genuine copy whose parent was deleted or is
invisible to you would be a **fabricated claim**. The measured trade over the live DB was:
**57 resolve · 12 go quiet · 0 lie.** Silence beats a confident wrong answer, and the guard that
produces it is load-bearing rather than defensive.
