Every new node's padding, margin and gap is drawn from the table above. Two consequences worth
stating because the obvious first draft violates both:

1. **The shape control's track is `p-1` and `gap-1` (4px), NOT FeatureVisibility's `p-0.5` /

   axes, not the inset.
2. **The grant switch's track is `h-4 w-7` (16 × 28px).** Both are multiples of 4 and neither is a
   new *value* — 16px and 28px are `h-4` and `w-7`, ordinary scale steps. The thumb is `h-3 w-3`,
   which is already in the census.

### Declared exceptions (both INHERITED — this phase extends neither)

1. **`text-[10.5px]` / `px-2.5` / `gap-2.5` = 10px steps.** The step panel's shipped dense rhythm.
   ⚠ **New nodes may reuse them at the SAME kind of site and may not put them at a new one.**
   `[SHIPPED]`
2. ⚠ **Every control on this surface is between 24px and 32px tall — far below the 44px touch
   guideline — and the grant switch inherits that, deliberately.** `h-6` / `h-7` / `h-8` are the
   shipped heights; a 44px switch would be the tallest control on a 400px panel and would read as
   the most important one, which it is not. **Mitigation, and it is required rather than
   optional:** the switch and its label are ONE `<button role="switch">` spanning the row's full
   width at `px-2 py-1` around a 16px track — a ~26px × ~370px target, not a 28px × 16px one.
   `[AUTO-RESOLVED]` Re-open trigger: *the first touch/mobile pass on the step panel, or the first
   phase permitted to re-baseline the panel's control heights.*

**Forbidden:** `p-0.5`, `gap-0.5`, `py-2.5`, `space-y-*`, and any arbitrary `[Npx]` spacing value.
A second rhythm is how one panel starts looking like two components.

### The write — REPLACE semantics, and the naive toggle is a lost-update bug

`[CONTEXT D-206.2-16]` `[RESEARCH §OQ#4]` `[PATTERNS §5 — FeatureVisibility.tsx:213-258]`

`connector_service.update_connection_grants` does `.update({"tool_grants": sanitized_grants})` —
**a whole-column overwrite.** A payload of `{ [tool]: next }` alone **wipes every other grant on the
connection.** ⚠ **This is the phase's single highest-consequence defect and it typechecks
perfectly.**

**Four parts, all copied from the one shipped UI toggle whose write sends a whole collection:**

1. **Send the FULL merged map, derived from the SERVER-OWNED PROP:**
   `updateConnectorGrants(connection.id, { ...(connection.tool_grants ?? {}), [tool]: next })`.
   ⚠ **`connection` is the prop off `ConnectionPicker`'s `read.connections`. NEVER a `useState`
   copy.**
2. **`if (busy) return` at the top of the handler** — the in-flight guard is what stops two
   concurrent PATCHes racing on one column.
3. **NO OPTIMISTIC FLIP.** The switch's position is the server's truth. It does not move on press;
   it moves when the row it renders from changes.
4. **A `✎` receipt on success (4000ms), a plain-language retry on failure** — never a silent one.

**Freshness — and this pass STATES WHICH ROUTE IS TAKEN, because `[PATTERNS §5]` requires it:**

> **ADOPT THE RESPONSE ROW.** `updateConnectorGrants` returns the full updated
> `ConnectorConnection` (projected through `_project`), so a new callback prop
> `onConnectionUpdated(row)` replaces the matching entry in `ConnectionPicker`'s
> `read.connections`. `bound` re-derives, the badge and the switch both move, and the **next**
> toggle merges from a fresh map.
>
> ⚠ **A re-fetch nonce is the rejected alternative and it is rejected for a measured reason:** it
> re-issues the whole connection list on every toggle inside a 400px panel field, and the response
> IS the row. ⚠ **But the hazard `[PATTERNS §5]` names is real either way — if the returned row is
> not written back into `read.connections`, the two go out of sync and the NEXT toggle reads a
> stale map.** The callback is not an optimisation; it is the thing that makes route (1) correct.
> `[AUTO-RESOLVED]` Re-open trigger: *a second writer of `tool_grants` appearing anywhere in the
> builder, or the first observed clobber.*

**It is still a read-modify-write over a client snapshot** — two admins in two browsers can clobber
each other. `tool_grants` carries no version column and adding one is a migration. **The honest
mitigation is the sentence, not a lock:** `MCP_GRANT_SCOPE_NOTE` says the permission is
connection-wide, which is the fact a second author needs.
