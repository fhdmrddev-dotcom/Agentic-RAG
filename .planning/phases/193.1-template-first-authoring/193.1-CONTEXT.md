<domain>
## Phase Boundary

**The AI drafts a workflow knowing what the template asks for.**

`POST /workflows/generate` has accepted `template_placeholders` since **Phase 103**; the frontend
has **never sent it** (`SEED-157`). Confirmed at discuss time, not inherited:
`frontend/src/pages/WorkflowBuilderPage.tsx:1268` sends `{describe, project_folder_id?}` and
nothing else. So a person who describes a workflow gets a draft built **blind to the template it
will have to fill**. Nothing errors — the retrieval steps are simply designed for the wrong
questions.

This phase closes the **re-opened half** of AUTH-03. `REQUIREMENTS.md:113` records the requirement
as *"ANSWERED WRONGLY, not delivered"*: Phase 193 shipped the **draft-then-attach** half (label +
conditionally hide the run-time upload box), and quick task `260814-q5r` added the placeholder read
on top of it. Both are **inputs** to this phase, not satisfactions of it.

**IN SCOPE**
- Supplying a template **before** describing, on the pre-draft describe screen.
- Reading that template's real placeholder keys and sending them as `template_placeholders` on the
  real `/generate` call.
- Binding those same bytes to the definition so **runs actually fill it** (D-05).
- The three-bucket name check for a template attached to an **already-drafted** workflow (SC#3).
- The G-5 extraction that makes room for all of the above (D-01).

**OUT OF SCOPE — named so nobody solves them here by accident**
- `SEED-158` — authoring placeholders INTO a plain template. A capability, not a copy fix.
- `SEED-159` — a null field rendering as a blank cell that lies, at **run** time.
- The govern door. It *is* the whole Builder; same limit sketches 164 and 165 both declared.
- A coverage **verdict** at attach time. Measured impossible — see D-09.

### The chicken-and-egg — how the template is read before a workflow exists

- **D-05 — THE STATELESS READ.** A new route takes the **bytes**, returns the **field names**, and
  **persists nothing**. Rejected: creating an empty draft up front (mints library rows the user
  never asked for, degrading exactly what Phases 192 and 192.1 spent two phases buying, and opens a
  second writer against 186-07's concurrency token) and stashing to a scratch Storage path (a new
  orphan-cleanup problem nobody owns, on a product already carrying RLS-sensitive template bytes).

  This matches the ROADMAP's recommendation on record **and** is the shape sketch 165's approved
  filled states already assume — the other two options would invalidate the approved mockup.

  **Cost, accepted with eyes open:** the `File` lives only in browser memory until save. A tab
  crash before save loses it and the author re-picks. Reading and binding are **two calls** — which
  is D-06's whole subject.

- **D-06 — AUTO-BIND ON FIRST SAVE. The run must fill the template the author supplied, not a
  second copy of it.** The held `File` is uploaded to the shipped `POST /workflows/{id}/template`
  the moment a `definition_id` exists. One file, one hand-off.

  ⚠ **Why this is a REQUIREMENT-level decision and not a nicety.** AUTH-03 as rewritten says two
  things: *"attaches a template when authoring it, **and every run fills that same template**"*.
  The stateless read delivers only the first. **None of the four ROADMAP success criteria mention
  the fill** — so a phase that stops at the read can pass all four criteria and still leave AUTH-03
  untickable. That is the exact trap Phase 193 fell into: built precisely to the written criteria,
  missed the requirement.

  ⚠ **Measured constraint, and it is why one call cannot do both.** `GenerateRequest`
  (`backend/app/api/workflows.py:1541-1547`) accepts `template_asset_id: UUID | None` **and**
  `template_placeholders: list[str] | None`. Asset ids are Storage **paths**, so
  `template_asset_id` is a measured **422** (`uuid_parsing`) from the browser — this is precisely
  why `260814-q5r` had to add a whole route rather than widen a query param. **`template_placeholders`
  is the ONLY wire that works from the frontend.**

  **Two rules that bind the implementation:**
  1. **The save must NEVER roll back on an upload failure.** A storage blip may not cost the author
     their draft.
  2. The bind is a **silent write the user never pressed a button for**, so its failure needs an
     honest line of its own. It may not be reported through the save's success path.

### The loading race

- **D-07 — DISABLE THE CTA WHILE THE READ IS IN FLIGHT.** Nothing today stops the author pressing
  **Write the first draft** mid-read, which sends `/generate` with no `template_placeholders` —
  the exact blind draft this phase exists to prevent, on a screen that just told them a template
  was attached. That is **worse** than not shipping the control: the screen made a promise and
  silently broke it, and the author cannot tell.

  Chosen because it makes the bad draft **impossible** rather than recoverable. Rejected: *let the
  draft wait for the read* (invisible, but "I pressed it and nothing happened yet" is harder to
  explain than "it's not ready yet") and *let it through and say so afterwards* (makes the phase's
  headline claim probabilistic and leans 167-C's admitted-heuristic safety net into being
  load-bearing for the main path).

- **D-08 — SC#4 holds BY CONSTRUCTION, not by a guard.** *Describing without a template is
  unchanged — the fast door stays fast.* No template attached ⇒ nothing to wait for ⇒ the CTA is
  never disabled. Planning must not add a conditional that could be got wrong; the absence of a
  read is the absence of a gate.

- **D-09 — D-07 REQUIRES 166-B's `loading` footing line to ship WITH it, not after it.** A button
  that is disabled and does not say why reads as broken. 166-B already carries a distinct footing
  value on all five reading arms including `loading`, so the answer has a home in the approved
  mockup — but the dependency is now a decision, not a hope.

### The late-attach reconcile (SC#3)

- **D-10 — 167-C: a three-bucket NAME check that explicitly disclaims being a coverage check.**
  Computed from the definition the page already holds, **no model call**. The three buckets are
  *produced by a step* · *supplied as a run input* · *named nowhere*.

  ⚠ **Three buckets, NEVER two — this is a red line, not a preference.** In sketch 167's fixture
  **2 of the 8** fields are run inputs: nothing produces them and nothing should. A two-bucket
  check lists them as gaps, and an author told twice that a correct workflow is broken stops
  reading the panel entirely.

  ⚠ **`reconcile.disclaim` is the single most important string on the surface.** Without it, C
  asserts a coverage verdict it **cannot compute** — the same class of dishonesty `SEED-159` is
  about, one surface earlier. **Measured, not assumed** (sketch 167 §"the measurement that
  constrains every variant"): at **attach** time the app has the placeholder keys, the draft's
  declared `output_keys`, phase slugs and `input_keys` — a *heuristic*. Only at **run** time does
  `check_coverage` (`backend/app/services/template_render_service.py:416-460`) produce
  `covered_keys` / `covers_template` / `null_leaf_count` — a *verdict*. The copy may say *"nothing
  in the steps **names** these"*. It may **never** say the template is uncovered.

- **D-11 — CAP THE RAIL, EXPAND IN PLACE.** Show the first few rows per bucket with a *"show all
  N"* that expands where it stands. **One home for one list.** Rejected: a modal past a threshold
  (two presentations of one list — the exact *"two homes drift"* argument that decided 165 — plus
  an unjustifiable threshold), the canvas (188.2 spent the card's badge and corner-mark budget
  deliberately; a third badge is a typecheck error, and this would fire G-5 on a **third** file in
  one phase), and an uncapped scrolling rail (puts the common small case inside a scroll region).

  This is also consistent with the ruling that already rejected **167-B**: it was rejected on
  **SIZE** because a second model call and a diff surface is too much build for a **safety net**.
  Spending a modal or a canvas surface on that same safety net would contradict it.

  **Cost, stated:** on the worst case — a 15-field template where 12 fields are named nowhere — the
  author expands to see the full picture. One extra click exactly where friction is least welcome.

- **D-12 — the reconcile is an EXPLICIT act and NEVER rewrites the draft.** Nothing edits a canvas
  the author has been working on without being asked. Not politeness: a reconcile that wrote the
  definition JSONB would be a **second writer**, which is the discipline Phase 186's `If-Match`
  token exists to protect.

## Specific Ideas

- **The one shape the three winners add up to** (operator, via `MANIFEST.md`): the pre-draft
  describe screen gains **one optional row** under the knowledge-base picker. **Ignore it and
  today's behaviour is byte-identical** — SC#4 by construction. Use it and the template's fields
  render **at the screen's own scale, as the spec the draft is aimed at**, with **one line
  underneath that always says what the draft will be built from**, taking a different value on each
  of the five reading arms.
- **165-B was rejected on a MEASUREMENT, not a taste** — mounting the shipped section as-is renders
  the fields at **10.5 px** under a **14 px** picker: the most consequential thing on the screen
  would also be the quietest. 165-C costs a `scale` prop on a component that shipped three weeks
  ago. This is the standing record of what *"just mount the shipped component"* actually costs.
- **SC#2 rides on one sentence** — 165's `spec.promise`: *"The draft will be built to fill these 8
  fields."* Everything else on screen says what the **template** contains; only this says what the
  **draft** is aimed at. **It states a count, so in build it MUST be derived from the list above it,
  never hardcoded.** An `8` beside a list of 5 is the exact class of silent lie this phase removes.
- **SC#2 must be proven on a REAL `/generate` call against a REAL template, never a stub.** The
  101.1 precedent: run `7fa36d2a` failed because a model that had never seen the placeholders
  invented its own key names.
- ⚠ **Test-data warning for UAT:** three of the operator's own workflows bind **SEED-prefixed**
  templates and correctly render *"could not read"*. Do not drive UAT with them.
- **Measured at sketch time, worth checking at UAT rather than discovering:** the describe column
  scrolls once the spec block is in it — `scrollHeight 761` vs `clientHeight 614` at the 660 px
  stage. The shipped grid already has `overflow-y-auto`, so this is real behaviour, not a defect —
  but the switch-to-govern strip may fall below the fold on a laptop viewport.
